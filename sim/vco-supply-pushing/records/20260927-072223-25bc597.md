- **Methodology / criteria / limitations**:
  - Per-point criterion, part 1 (plumbing): ngspice exits 0, prints the harness's analysis-completion marker (`sim/harness: analysis complete`, echoed by the injected `.control` block -- ngspice does not print its own "Total analysis time" banner for a control-driven analysis), and emits no `Error:` line.
  - Per-point criterion, part 2 (measurement): the measured node `v(clk)` oscillates -- at least 4 rising crossings of 0.5*VDD after 60 ns of settling. A swept point that does not is reported as **no oscillation**, with no frequency attributed to it. A characterization sweep deliberately runs past the edges of the tuning range, so an individual dead swept value is recorded as data rather than gating the point; this manifest instead requires at least 1 of the swept values to oscillate.
  - Measurement method: the harness injects the analysis itself (an ngspice `.control` block built by `sim/harness/measure.py`), `linearize`s `v(clk)` onto the transient's own 50p grid and `wrdata`-dumps it; the reducer then extracts hysteresis-guarded, linearly-interpolated threshold crossings at 0.5*VDD (the threshold tracks each point's own swept supply, not the nominal rail) and derives frequency, duty cycle and time-to-lock from them. The dumps themselves are **not** committed -- `sim/README.md`'s retention policy treats waveform data as regenerable from the frozen netlist plus the logged environment.
  - Not measured here: loop bandwidth and phase margin are open-loop quantities and need their own AC/linearized-model testbench -- see `sim/harness/measure.py`'s module docstring for why they are deliberately out of this reducer's scope, and `sim/loop-ac/` for the campaign that does measure them.
  - DUT / limitations: this testbench's DUT is design/vco/vco_ring5.sch driven open-loop (no PFD, charge pump, loop filter or divider in the path) -- it characterizes the VCO block alone, exactly as sim/vco does, and says nothing about the assembled loop's in-lock rail rejection, which is a separate transient campaign (issue #166). VCTRL is held FIXED at each of four operating points (0.8, 0.9, 1.2, 1.5 V) and the supply is the measured independent variable, supplied by the corner runner's own 1.62/1.80/1.98 V axis: the frequency-vs-VDD pushing figure is therefore a CROSS-POINT derivation over the three supply rows that share a (corner, temperature, VCTRL) triple, not a within-point slope, and the harness's own per-point tuning-summary table (which reports MHz per volt of the swept VCTRL axis) is not that figure -- see sim/vco-supply-pushing/analysis/pushing.py, which derives it from the record's own swept-axis table and appends it to the record at mint time. All four VCTRL values sit inside the free-running span sim/vco/records/20260904-163130-f3ae976.md measured (all 45 of its points oscillated at all six of its swept values), so unlike sim/vco -- a characterization sweep that deliberately runs past the edge of the tuning range, where a dead swept value is data -- a dead swept value here would leave a (corner, temperature, VCTRL) triple with no pushing figure at all: min_oscillating_points is therefore the full 4, and a non-oscillating swept value fails its point. The four values are chosen to resolve the pushing figure as a function of bias rather than report one number smuggled out of a single operating point, because the two mechanisms that set it have OPPOSITE signs and their sum changes sign inside this range. 0.8 V is the bottom of the characterized tuning range, where the starving branch is weakest and closest to the supply-independent-current idealization DR-006's `1/VDD` floor argument assumes; 0.9 V is the operating point nearest the closed-loop target (sim/pll-lock drives N=25 against a 10 MHz reference for 250 MHz, and sim/vco measured 318.1 MHz at tt/27 degC/1.80 V/VCTRL=0.9 V, the characterized point closest to that target); 1.2 V and 1.5 V walk the current-dominated upper half of the usable span, where the sum has flipped sign, with 1.5 V carrying the largest pushing magnitude of the four -- the value that bounds a future row-13 AC budget from above. Each swept point is a fresh 120 ns transient started from RING0 forced low with `uic` (the ring's DC equilibrium is the unstable all-stages-at-midrail point), the first 60 ns discarded as startup. Step (50p) and settle (60n) match sim/vco's exactly, so the same steady-state frequency is measured the same way and the two campaigns' shared (corner, temperature, supply, VCTRL) rows are directly comparable as an independent reproduction; tran_stop is 120 ns rather than sim/vco's 200 ns, halving the per-transient cost at no measurement cost (the 60 ns retained window still holds 13+ cycles at the slowest point measured here, versus the 4 edges min_edges requires) -- a record states the cross-check against sim/vco explicitly rather than asserting comparability. The reducer's threshold tracks each point's OWN supply (0.5 * VDD, sim/harness/measure.py), which is load-bearing here in a way it is not for a fixed-rail sweep: a threshold pinned to the nominal rail would fold a supply-dependent measurement artefact straight into the reported %/V. timeout_s is 7200 rather than sim/vco's 3600 because this grid is meant to be run with sim/run_corners.py -j on a shared host, where per-point wall clock stretches with contention. process_corners is the ratified rows 19/20 PVT grid (spec/target-spec.md, DR-003, #77).
